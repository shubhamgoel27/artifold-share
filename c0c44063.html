01

Problem Statement

  • Audience and customer. Advertisers (more relevant delivery per ad dollar), end users (better-matched ads), and internally the Instagram Ads Ranking org, since the ranking model is shared infrastructure and a new feature family there is an org-level asset.
  • Why it is critical. Short-form video ads are extremely text-rich: offers, calls-to-action, prices, brand and product mentions overlaid on the creative. The ranker consumed video ads mostly through user-side signals, engagement sequences, and dense visual embeddings; the text on the creative itself was largely absent. NE improvements on this last-stage ranker compound to large impact at Instagram scale.
  • Insight that prompted the work. A legacy signal touched this, but it ran an ancient OCR model on the static thumbnail only, and even that limited version was a high-importance feature. That told us the modality was valuable and under-exploited. A later clean single-thumbnail experiment confirmed coverage, not modeling, was the bottleneck.
  • My role. I led the overall initiative from the IG Ads Ranking team and am first author on the resulting RecSys paper. Shipping a ranking feature at this scale is inherently cross-team: three partner teams built the upstream models (an OCR team for recognition, a video infra team for frame extraction, a text-models team for the TextRay embedding). I set the thesis, the requirements, and the quality bar for each partner component, and I personally owned the ranking-side work: feature engineering into the ranker (TF-IDF, the RQ-VAE quantization, and integration), the ablation methodology, the online experimentation, and the write-up.
  • What was uniquely challenging.
    • OCR had to run at billion-ad daily throughput, which rules out general-purpose vision-language models on cost.
    • These are videos, so you cannot OCR every frame; frame selection matters.
    • The extracted text had to land in the ranker's existing sparse categorical embedding tables, not as a dense feature needing its own tower and serving budget.
    • It required orchestrating four teams toward one shared metric.
  • Intended outcome. A production, NE-positive feature family, and a reusable recipe for bringing any frame-level perceptual modality into a tabular CTR ranker.
02

Solutions Explored

  • Baseline. The production short-form video CTR ranker, including the legacy OCR signal, so every gain had to be incremental over an existing feature.
  • Three design decisions, each with a real alternative.
    • OCR model. A vision-language model reading the creative directly is highest quality, but its throughput cannot serve billions of ads a day at acceptable cost. We went with a specialized OCR model (Ads-OCR, built by the OCR team to my requirement of near-VLM quality at serving-viable throughput): about 93% of the VLM's word-level F1 at roughly 80× the throughput. Decision rule: quality-per-throughput.
    • Frame extraction. Uniform sampling either misses text-rich frames or wastes compute on near-duplicates. The video infra team built a smart extractor that reuses a production frame-CTR scoring model (so the quality score is essentially free), plus SSIM dedup and adaptive sampling. It recovers more OCR text at half the frame budget.
    • Feature representation (my core work). Three families, evaluated against each other: TF-IDF tokens (exact keywords), the TextRay dense sentence embedding (contextual meaning, from the text-models team), and a 3-byte RQ-VAE semantic-ID code I derived by quantizing that embedding so it fits the ranker's categorical tables.
  • Evaluation framework. Tiered: OCR word-F1, then a representation ablation on 200,000 ads with four independent lenses (LLM-as-judge, plus judge-free deterministic anchors and a human inter-rater check), then offline ranker NE per feature, then an online A/B, all gated on per-ad serving cost.
  • How I converged, and what was contentious. The result inverted the expected serving design. The 3-byte codes recovered nearly all of the dense embedding's ranking value, and adding the dense embedding on top of the bundle was within run-to-run noise at 500× the storage. So we dropped the dense tower and launched TF-IDF plus the codes. Sign-off was straightforward; the proposal looked like modest ROI going in, and the notable surprise was the magnitude, concentrated on short-form video surfaces.

03

Technical Considerations

  • Quality vs throughput. Specialized OCR at roughly 80× a VLM's throughput is what makes per-frame perception affordable at billion-ad daily scale.
  • Coverage vs compute (video). Smart frame selection recovers more OCR text at half the per-video frame budget.
  • Representation cost vs quality. 3-byte RQ-VAE codes versus a 1536-byte dense vector. The codes integrate with the ranker's existing categorical embedding tables, adding no new dense matmul at serving time.
  • Integration and build-vs-reuse. All OCR features are added to the ad-side tower; crosses with user and context features are handled by the ranker's existing interaction layers (FM-style crosses, DCN, cross-attention), so integration cost stayed low and feature value was measurable through the standard ablation pipeline.
  • Robustness. OCR tokens are confidence-filtered (≥ 0.6) upstream so noise never reaches the ranker; missing features degrade cleanly.
  • Architecture notes. Ads-OCR: a three-stage detect-crop-recognize pipeline (ConvNeXt detector with rotated boxes, fixed-size cropper, TrOCR for Latin and LSTM for non-Latin) spanning 41 languages. RQ-VAE: three residual codebooks of 256 entries, trained with reconstruction, VQ commitment, and sparsity losses, with care taken around a codebook-utilization pitfall under the straight-through estimator.

  • How success was determined. Offline NE on a retrained treatment arm (training config, data window, and architecture held identical to baseline) over a ~120-billion-example window, stabilizing into a tight band after about 30 billion examples. Online, a multi-week A/B at 80% traffic, evaluated with a one-sided z-test and cluster-robust standard errors at the ad-id level (impressions of the same ad are not independent). The feature is in production.
  • How data informed iteration. Per-feature offline ablation guided the launch. The four-lens representation ablation triangulated which family carried the most signal. A single-thumbnail experiment (NE only 0.01 to 0.015%) showed the bottleneck was modality coverage, not feature engineering, and justified investing in per-frame video OCR.

05

Key Learnings

1
The high-yield part of a rich modality can be tiny and categorical. I expected the dense embedding to dominate; the 3-byte codes recovered nearly all of it at 500× less storage, and the dense tower was within noise. I now default to quantizing to a categorical feature first and making the dense version earn its serving cost before shipping it.
2
Coverage beats cleverness when the modality is under-sampled. The single-thumbnail result (0.01 to 0.015% NE) versus full-video frames (0.06%) was a large swing from capturing more of the signal, not better modeling. It reset how I scope new-signal work: secure coverage first.
3
Leading a four-team initiative is a requirements-and-metric problem. The orchestration mattered more than any single model. Crisp, falsifiable requirements and one shared metric (NE) let each partner team optimize independently while I owned the integration and the outcome.
